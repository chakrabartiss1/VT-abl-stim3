Teaching notes: criteria and caveats
  • PPI–VTCL is most useful when entrainment is demonstrated and the tachycardia resumes with interpretable timing. Fusion, capture, pacing site and decremental conduction can affect interpretation.
  • Concealed fusion may occur when pacing from within a protected circuit, but is not by itself proof of an obligatory circuit site. Manifest fusion may reflect interaction with the paced wavefront, including direct capture of adjacent tissue.
  • Stimulus-to-QRS latency can help characterize a protected channel when interpreted with local electrograms, activation sequence and pacing response. Avoid universal timing cut-offs that ignore anatomy and conduction properties.
  • Late potentials and fractionated electrograms are substrate clues, not automatic proof of critical-isthmus participation.
  • Clinical mapping requires integration of entrainment responses, activation mapping, pace mapping where appropriate, capture assessment, hemodynamics and the full clinical context.
